Tokenized deposits for Swiss banking

Trace a tokenized deposit through payment and settlement.

A tokenized deposit is still a CHF claim on its issuing bank. Tokenization changes the representation and transfer workflow. It does not answer the hard questions about authority, finality, recovery, and reconciliation.

01 / What changes

The deposit stays a bank liability. Its operating record changes.

A token can be an instruction, a representation of a deposit, or the authoritative holder record. The simulator separates those designs so a wallet, token, ledger, and customer claim do not become the same thing by accident.

02 / Four representations

The token can mean four different things.

These models keep the payment objective constant and change the claim, the authoritative record, and the settlement design. The simulator uses the same four definitions below.

  1. BASELINEPayment instructionThe token carries an instruction. CBS remains authoritative for the customer balance and bank liability.
  2. TOKENIZED DEPOSITMirrored depositA DLT token represents the bank deposit. CBS and GL remain the liability control records.
  3. TOKENIZED DEPOSITLedger-native depositThe DLT holder record runs the balance. The bank still controls issuance, recovery, and the issuer perimeter.
  4. COMPARATORNon-bank stablecoinThe holder faces a separate issuer, reserve, and redemption arrangement. It is not a bank deposit.

03 / Why consider it

Use a token only when the workflow changes.

Switzerland already has SIC and Instant Payments for domestic account payments. A bank should add a token only when shared state, conditional release, or coordinated cash-and-asset settlement removes a measured hand-off, risk, or reconciliation cost.

  1. 01
    Shared workflow stateParticipants see one controlled status instead of inferring it from messages and local records.
  2. 02
    Conditional cashValue remains locked until accepted evidence, an expiry, or a defined repair path releases it.
  3. 03
    Coordinated settlementCash and another obligation can complete under one agreed workflow, with the settlement anchor stated explicitly.
  4. 04
    Keep the existing rail when it is enoughThe token is not the benefit. The workflow is.

04 / What the bank must decide

The design starts with the claim.

These are product and operating choices. A shared ledger cannot decide them after the fact.

  1. 01Who is the debtor?Name the bank or separate issuer that owes the holder.
  2. 02Which record decides a dispute?State whether CBS, DLT, or another controlled record is authoritative.
  3. 03What creates finality?Separate workflow completion, ledger posting, accounting recognition, and legal effect.
  4. 04How do institutions settle?Identify the institutional cash leg and the point at which it discharges.
  5. 05How does the bank recover?Define issuance, controls, reconciliation, pause, repair, and customer communication.
  6. 06Which model fits the use case?The simulator holds the payment flow constant and changes these answers.

05 / Swiss reference map

Each project answers a different question.

Use these as scoped reference points. They do not approve or prescribe a bank-specific tokenized deposit.

  • SIC and Instant PaymentsDomestic payment railProduction

    SIC is Switzerland's domestic CHF payment infrastructure. Eligible participants settle interbank obligations in sight deposits at the SNB. SIC Instant Payments adds 24/7 account-to-account payments.

    Neither system creates or runs a customer tokenized deposit. They remain the settlement baseline for a bank that keeps the CBS and GL authoritative.

  • Project AgoráCross-border wholesale testControlled real-value test

    Agorá tests cross-border wholesale payments with a unifying ledger for platform-authoritative tokenized commercial-bank deposits and jurisdictional ledgers for tokenized central-bank reserves. Participant middleware keeps confirmation of payee, route choice, pricing, and compliance checks private.

    Shared contracts coordinate validation, locks, delegated authority, and settlement. Its platform-as-record model differs from a CBS-authoritative mirrored deposit. Controlled real-value testing does not establish production resilience, a final rulebook, or a Swiss bank product approval.

  • Project HelvetiaWholesale settlement pilotPilot in production infrastructure

    Helvetia tests tokenized-asset settlement in central-bank money through two paths. One uses wholesale CBDC on SIX Digital Asset Platform. The other synchronizes a DLT transaction with an RTGS payment in SIC.

    It examines the wholesale settlement leg between eligible institutions. It does not issue a retail CBDC, define the customer's deposit claim, or commit the SNB to permanent wholesale CBDC.

  • BX DigitalDLT securities settlementProduction for approved DLT-securities scope

    BX Digital is a DLT trading and settlement infrastructure for its approved securities scope. It links securities transfers on its DLT infrastructure with RTGS settlement in central-bank money through SIC.

    The cash movement remains between eligible institutions' SNB accounts. This is a production example of DLT securities settlement connected to SIC, not a tokenized-deposit scheme or approval for one.
